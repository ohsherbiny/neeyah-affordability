Sell before month 180 and two different questions get asked at once. This separates them. Companion to the Scenario Explorer, same engine. Planning tool, not evidence — the contracts are the source.

Scenario

The purchase

Appreciation — two rates, not one

Both sliders are how fast the house gains value each year. The contract tracks two values for the same house. The one that prices your buyouts can only rise 5% a year; the other follows the real market with no limit. So in a hot market you buy slices at the slower number and sell at the real one, and the gap is yours. Both fall without limit. Leave them equal unless you want to see that gap.

What a slice of the house costs you to buy. Upkeep follows the real market value instead, because a real roof costs what a real roof costs.

What the house really fetches. The agreement splits sale proceeds against this, not against the capped figure.

Above +5% they separate: you buy increments at the capped number and sell at market.

Carrying costs

Not the same as the two rent-growth sliders above. Those are the place you would rent instead, for the "was buying worth it" test. This one is the rent you actually pay Neeyah, on the share they still own. It is capped at 5%/yr, which is why this slider stops there and the other two go to 8%.

Unresolved. Identical at a steady rate; the two readings separate only after a hot year, so set one below.

Default 1.1% is a rough estimate, not a quote. Repairs also do not arrive smoothly: a furnace is not $200 a month, it is $7,000 in one go. ⚠️ Neeyah's share of the shared repairs is already taken out of this number, so do not reduce it again for your ownership.

0 = no association. 100% yours, however much of the house you own. Dues, late fees, penalties and special assessments all sit with the resident, so at 20% owned you pay 20% of the tax bill but all of a special assessment.

How fast you buy equity

From 20% down, 5.33%/yr is what reaches 100% at month 180. A flat 5% reaches only 95%. The agreement caps acceleration at 10%/yr.

Versus renting

All-in cost of having owned = everything you ever paid, minus what came back. That is the money you put in, plus rent, tax, insurance and upkeep, minus what you walk away with. Rent, tax, insurance and upkeep never come back. They bought you housing, so they count in full here and stay out of the capital test above.
